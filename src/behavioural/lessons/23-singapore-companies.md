# Singapore companies: GovTech, Grab, DBS, ByteDance and Sea

## Before this lesson

Read [Building a story bank](#/lesson/behavioural/story-bank), [Tell me about yourself, Why us and Why this role](#/lesson/behavioural/tell-me-about-yourself) and [Hypothetical and scenario questions](#/lesson/behavioural/hypothetical-questions). The previous three lessons covered large US-based companies; this one covers five employers that hire many software engineers in Singapore.

## In plain words

Singapore's technology job market is a mix. There are regional offices of global companies (the previous three lessons), home-grown regional technology companies such as Grab and Sea, banks that run large engineering teams such as DBS, the government's own technology agency GovTech, and large companies headquartered elsewhere in Asia with major Singapore offices, such as ByteDance. Each publishes its own values and hiring guidance, and they differ in useful ways.

A word of caution before the details: you will find many articles online about a single "Singapore interview style" or "Asian interview culture". This track's research found no reputable source for such generalisations, so this lesson does not offer one. Instead, it uses what each company says about itself, and labels anything else as general advice. The underlying skills are the same as everywhere else in this track: clear STAR stories, specific evidence, honest reflection and research into the company.

Why it matters for a job: each of these employers names its values and says it checks for them. If you walk into a GovTech interview with stories mapped to its values, or into a Grab interview with examples of its 4Hs, you are answering the questions they told you they would ask.

## GovTech

GovTech, the Government Technology Agency of Singapore, builds digital services for the government.

### What GovTech says about its process

GovTech's "How we hire" page (at the time of writing) describes three steps:

1. **Apply** through Careers@Gov, the Singapore public service job portal.
2. **Interview**: if you are shortlisted, a recruiter arranges an interview with the hiring manager and possibly future teammates.
3. **Decision**: the hiring team assesses candidates against established frameworks and criteria set in advance for the role, and makes an offer to the selected candidate.

The page says GovTech assesses **competencies**, **problem-solving ability** and **compatibility with its values**. Its interview tips are to research the scope of the job, to explain how you break down challenges so your critical thinking shows, and to show how you embody its values. Its CV tips include quantifying results and explaining the value you added. It also suggests reviewing your public social media and setting up a professional profile.

### GovTech's values

GovTech's values are **Agile, Bold and Collaborative**, which it calls its ABC values.

| Value | Story types that fit |
|---|---|
| Agile | Adapting to changing priorities, small reversible steps (lesson 14) |
| Bold | Taking initiative, proposing a better way, calculated risks (lessons 13 and 15) |
| Collaborative | Teamwork across teams and with non-technical users (lesson 10) |

### Preparing for GovTech

- Map three stories to the ABC values, one each.
- Practise **thinking aloud** through a problem: GovTech asks you to show how you break down challenges, so in both behavioural and technical answers, state the steps of your reasoning.
- Prepare a "Why GovTech?" answer about building services that citizens use, with a specific digital service you have used and something you noticed about it.

## Grab

Grab is a Southeast Asian technology company offering ride-hailing, deliveries and financial services.

### What Grab says about its process

Grab's general "How we hire" page (at the time of writing) describes:

- **Functional and behavioural interviews** covering your background, career goals and how your skills fit the role, with questions about handling challenges, managing stakeholders and driving results.
- **Practical assessments**, depending on the role: case studies, portfolio reviews, technical tasks, or take-home exercises followed by a presentation.
- A **culture-fit** assessment against Grab's values, which it calls the **4Hs**: **Heart, Hunger, Honour and Humility**.
- For **leadership roles**, a closer look at management style: how you inspire, collaborate and guide others, with examples of leading, mentoring and making decisions.

Grab's tips include: know your craft; show your problem-solving process, not only results; show curiosity about emerging trends, since Grab describes itself as AI-first; know the values and prepare examples of them; for behavioural questions, highlight your individual contribution and its impact; and understand the business well enough to propose solutions aligned with its mission in Southeast Asia.

### Grab's 4Hs

| Value | In plain words (paraphrase) | Story types that fit |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | Caring about the people you serve and work with | Customer focus (lesson 16), helping teammates (lesson 10) |
| Hunger | Drive to achieve and improve | Ownership and results (lesson 13) |
| Honour | Integrity, doing the right thing | Failure owned honestly (lesson 12), raising a risk (lesson 11) |
| Humility | Openness to learning, credit to others | Feedback and reflection (lesson 12), credit in teamwork stories |

The paraphrases above are written for this lesson; read Grab's own pages for its wording.

### An older view from Grab's engineering blog

A 2017 post on Grab's engineering blog described how Grab hired engineers in Singapore at that time. Because it is several years old, treat it as history, not current practice. It described a process of a recruiter phone screen, an online coding round, technical rounds and a final round with several engineers, taking a few weeks; said that culture fit was critical; mentioned the principle "Your problem is my problem" as a sign of how much Grab valued helping colleagues; noted that candidates would work with colleagues of more than 30 nationalities; and said senior candidates were asked about their technical decisions and how they would hire and build a team. The current page above supersedes it wherever they differ.

### Preparing for Grab

- One story per H, with your individual contribution clear.
- A stakeholder-management story (lesson 15) and a "driving results" story (lesson 13), since Grab names both.
- A "Why Grab?" answer grounded in a specific Southeast Asian user problem.
- For senior roles, stories about hiring, mentoring and decisions (lesson 24).

## DBS

DBS is a Singapore-headquartered bank with large technology teams.

DBS's "How we hire" page tells candidates to **expect scenario-based questions**, and says DBS runs **pre-employment checks** before confirming a hire. DBS's page gives less detail than the others in this lesson, so check it, and your recruiter's instructions, for the current steps. A **pre-employment check** is verification of facts such as your identity, qualifications and employment history before you join.

### Preparing for DBS

- **Scenario questions**: use the six-step chain from [lesson 17](#/lesson/behavioural/hypothetical-questions): clarify, priorities, options, decide, communicate, evidence. In a bank, scenarios often involve customers' money, data and regulation, so name those priorities explicitly.
- **Accuracy on your CV**: since facts are checked, make sure every date, title and qualification you state is exact.
- **Discretion**: as in [lesson 22](#/lesson/behavioural/microsoft-apple), banking employers are likely to notice how you handle confidential information in your stories.

A **fictional** banking-style scenario to practise: "You notice a batch job has been sending duplicate payment notifications to about 200 customers since this morning. Your lead is on leave. What do you do?" Apply the chain: clarify whether any money moved or only messages; priority on customers' trust and correct records; options (stop the job, let it finish, notify customers); decide and sequence; communicate to the on-call manager, support and, if needed, the customers; and close with a real example of similar judgement if you have one.

## ByteDance

ByteDance, which runs products including TikTok, has a large engineering presence in Singapore.

### What ByteDance says about its process

ByteDance's interview page (at the time of writing) says:

- Most positions involve **several interviews**.
- You may be invited to an **assessment** combining tasks and activities, virtual or in person, possibly lasting a few hours.
- Interviews cover your professional background, related experience and why you want to join; you are encouraged to let your personality and experience come through.
- To prepare, review the job description, learn about ByteDance, and research recent news, trends, competitors, company history and opportunities in its sector.
- **Assessment tips**: share your thoughts and ideas; let other participants speak and draw them into group discussion; avoid dwelling on negatives; make sure you understand each task.

An **assessment centre** (the general term for this kind of session) is a set of exercises, often including group discussions, where several observers watch how you work with others as well as what you produce. The ByteDance tips map directly onto the teamwork and influence skills in lessons 10 and 15: contributing, listening and bringing others in are all being observed.

### ByteStyle

ByteDance's culture page lists six values it calls **ByteStyle**. Titles are ByteDance's; descriptions are paraphrases.

| ByteStyle value | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Always Day 1 | Keep a start-up spirit: innovate, stay nimble, avoid complacency |
| Champion Diversity and Inclusion | Value different strengths and perspectives; assume good intent |
| Be Candid and Clear | Speak openly about problems, communicate plainly, use data |
| Seek Truth and Be Pragmatic | Think independently, go to first-hand facts, focus on real impact |
| Be Courageous and Aim for the Highest | Take calculated risks, consider broad options, execute excellently |
| Grow Together | Stay committed through setbacks, solve problems together, keep learning |

### Preparing for ByteDance

- Map stories to ByteStyle; "Seek Truth and Be Pragmatic" fits Dive Deep-style stories (lesson 20), "Be Candid and Clear" fits conflict and feedback stories.
- Practise group exercises with friends: one person observes and notes who listened, who built on others' ideas and who brought quiet people in.
- Research recent news and the sector before the interview, as ByteDance advises.

## Sea

Sea is a Singapore-headquartered company whose businesses include e-commerce, digital entertainment and digital financial services.

Sea's values page lists five values. Titles are Sea's; descriptions are paraphrases.

| Value | In plain words | Story types that fit |
|---|---|---|
| We Serve | Customers decide what our products are worth; meet unmet needs | Customer focus (lesson 16) |
| We Adapt | Change is constant; welcome and lead it | Ambiguity and changing priorities (lesson 14) |
| We Run | Move faster and with more urgency, under shifting pressure | Delivery under deadlines (lesson 13) |
| We Commit | Dedication to values, customers, partners and each other | Ownership, follow-through (lesson 13) |
| We Stay Humble | Stay humble despite growth | Feedback, credit to others (lessons 10 and 12) |

## Five companies, one story bank

All five name values and say, in some form, that they check for them. You do not need a separate story bank per company; you need one good bank and a mapping. A **fictional** example of one story mapped five ways: you noticed that a clinic's appointment app failed for older users at the verification step, sat with support, found the cause and fixed it with the owning team (lesson 16).

| Company | Value to aim at | Headline emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| GovTech | Collaborative | Working with the support team and the owning team |
| Grab | Heart | Care for older users who were failing to pay |
| DBS | (scenario follow-up) | How you would handle it if money had been affected |
| ByteDance | Seek Truth and Be Pragmatic | Going to first-hand facts by listening to calls |
| Sea | We Serve | Users decide what the product is worth; meeting an unmet need |

## Pitfalls

1. **Relying on generalisations about "Asian interview culture".** Use each company's own guidance.
2. **Ignoring the values pages.** All five publish values; several say they assess them.
3. **Treating old blog posts as current.** Grab's 2017 post is useful history only.
4. **Inaccurate CV details** where pre-employment checks are run.
5. **Dominating group exercises.** ByteDance's tips ask you to bring others in.
6. **A generic "Why us?"** These companies serve specific regional users; mention them specifically.

## Your turn

1. Choose two of the five companies. For each, map your story bank to its values and find the gap.
2. Write a "Why this company?" answer for each, with one specific product or service you have used.
3. Practise the DBS-style scenario above using the six-step chain, in under three minutes.
4. Run a 20-minute group-discussion practice with two or three friends and ask an observer to note who brought others in.

## Summary

- Singapore's market mixes global, regional, banking, government and Asian-headquartered employers. There is no reliable source for a single "Singapore interview style"; use each company's guidance.
- GovTech: apply via Careers@Gov; interview with hiring manager and possibly teammates; assessment against pre-set criteria; competencies, problem solving and ABC values (Agile, Bold, Collaborative).
- Grab: functional and behavioural interviews on challenges, stakeholders and results; role-dependent practical assessments; culture fit on the 4Hs (Heart, Hunger, Honour, Humility); a 2017 engineering post is dated history.
- DBS: expect scenario-based questions; pre-employment checks.
- ByteDance: several interviews, possibly a multi-hour assessment with group work; research the company and sector; six ByteStyle values starting with "Always Day 1".
- Sea: five values: We Serve, We Adapt, We Run, We Commit, We Stay Humble.
- One story bank, mapped to each company's values, is enough.

## Glossary

- **GovTech**: the Government Technology Agency of Singapore.
- **Careers@Gov**: the Singapore public service job portal.
- **ABC values**: GovTech's values, Agile, Bold and Collaborative.
- **4Hs**: Grab's values, Heart, Hunger, Honour and Humility.
- **ByteStyle**: ByteDance's six core values.
- **Pre-employment check**: verification of facts such as identity, qualifications and work history before a hire is confirmed.
- **Assessment centre**: a set of exercises, often including group work, observed by assessors.
- **Culture fit**: how well a candidate's way of working matches a company's stated values.
